01 · the challenge

Making CRM feel more approachable for small-businesses

Starter Suite was designed to give small businesses an easier entry point into Salesforce, but the category language around CRM could still feel abstract or overly enterprise-oriented. The product-marketing challenge was to make the value recognizable to an SMB owner in seconds: less about software architecture, more about the everyday work of managing customers, leads, and growth.

At the same time, the campaign had to behave like native social content. We needed creator-led stories that could earn attention on TikTok, Meta, and YouTube without losing the product message or the path toward trial and adoption.

02 · my role

From product positioning to campaign execution

I helped translate Starter Suite positioning into creator briefs, social concepts, scripts, and channel-ready campaign messaging. I coordinated two agencies and five creators from briefing through production and approvals, working across Growth, Paid Media, Legal, Brand, and Localization to keep the work accurate, on-brand, and usable across markets.

I also handled the connective tissue between creative and measurement: CTA and UTM QA, launch tracking, performance monitoring, and post-launch reporting. That let us evaluate the work as a funnel program rather than as a collection of social posts.

03 · the work

Social-first product storytelling

The creative strategy started with recognizable small-business moments instead of product terminology. Each creator could make the story feel personal, but the briefs anchored the content in a consistent set of product benefits and audience problems so the campaign still laddered back to Starter Suite positioning.

That balance mattered. The social execution needed enough flexibility to feel native to each creator and channel, while the underlying message still had to answer the same product-marketing question: why should an SMB customer care about this product now?

04 · how I approached it

Starting with the audience

I used TikTok, Reddit, and broader social research to understand how small-business and AI audiences described their problems, what hooks felt overused, and which formats were earning attention. I turned those observations into messaging recommendations, creator briefs, and scripts rather than starting from internal product language alone.

During production, I managed feedback across internal teams and external partners, then worked with Paid Media on amplification and measurement. The goal was to create a learning loop where audience response could inform the next creative decision instead of treating launch as the end of the process.

05 · impact

Reach was only part of the story

The campaign generated more than 2M views and contributed to 100+ prospective SMB customers. More importantly, trial-to-paid conversion increased by 24%, giving us a stronger signal that the work was influencing behavior beyond awareness.

Channel performance reinforced the same story: YouTube view-through rate improved 48% and Meta click-through rate improved 37%. Those results helped show that a clearer, more audience-native product story could improve both attention and downstream intent.

06 · what I learned

Stories work best when audiences see themselves in them

The strongest content did not begin with a feature. It began with a problem the audience already recognized, then introduced the product as a useful next step. That principle has carried directly into my later PMM work: understand the audience first, build the message around their context, and use performance data to keep sharpening the story.
