01 · the challenge

Making technical products easier to understand

Agentforce and Starter Suite served very different buyers, but both presented the same product-marketing problem: turning a broad set of technical capabilities into a story customers could understand quickly. Agentforce needed to make an emerging AI category feel concrete and trustworthy, while Starter Suite needed to make CRM feel useful and accessible to small businesses that might not think in enterprise-software language.

My focus was clarity without flattening the product. The messaging needed to explain the value proposition, connect features to recognizable use cases, and give customers a clear next step while staying consistent with the broader Salesforce launch narrative.

02 · my role

Connecting product, message, and launch

I worked across the customer-facing and internal sides of the launch story. I wrote and refined web headlines, body copy, CTAs, creator scripts, product walkthroughs, launch recaps, campaign briefs, and sales-facing materials, then carried those assets through review with Product Marketing, Growth, Web, Legal, Brand, Localization, Paid Media, and Sales.

That cross-functional process was part of the strategy. My job was not only to write clean copy, but to keep the audience, value proposition, use cases, and calls to action aligned as the story moved from product priorities into web, campaign, and enablement touchpoints.

03 · Starter Suite

Turning product value into customer-facing copy

For Starter Suite, I centered the web story on the jobs small-business customers were already trying to accomplish rather than on CRM architecture. I worked on headlines, body copy, CTAs, and supporting messaging tied to everyday SMB use cases, with an emphasis on making the product feel approachable, useful, and easy to act on.

The same positioning carried into creator and growth work, giving the broader campaign a consistent product story as customers moved from social discovery to the Salesforce site and toward trial.

04 · Agentforce

Making AI feel more concrete

For Agentforce, the messaging challenge was to make agentic AI feel less abstract. I supported customer-facing copy that moved from capability language into practical outcomes, use cases, and product walkthroughs so visitors could understand not only what Agentforce was, but where it could fit into real work.

I also paid close attention to consistency across web and launch materials, because a new category is easier to understand when customers and internal teams encounter the same core value proposition repeatedly.

05 · launch enablement

Giving teams a consistent story to work from

I also built launch recaps, product walkthroughs, campaign briefs, one-pagers, and sales-facing materials that consolidated the audience, value proposition, use cases, proof points, and campaign priorities into a story teams could reuse. That helped connect the external message to downstream Sales and Growth execution instead of treating each asset as a separate piece of copy.

For me, that was the most product-marketing part of the work: creating a message architecture that could travel across teams and channels without losing the reason the product mattered.

06 · what I learned

Clarity shapes the product experience

This work reinforced that messaging is part of the product experience. Clear positioning reduces the amount of interpretation a customer has to do, while strong enablement gives Sales, Growth, and channel teams a consistent story to carry forward.

It also clarified the kind of product marketing I want to keep doing: work that sits between product understanding, audience insight, writing, and GTM execution.
